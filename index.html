Tips for Financial Cybersecurity

Protecting your accounts, assets, and identity across digital banking.

In an increasingly digital world, safeguarding your banking and personal data is vital. Cyber threats like phishing, identity theft, and unauthorized account access continue to evolve. Follow these practical, everyday cybersecurity practices to keep your money and identity secure.

1. Create Strong, Unique Passwords

Avoid using predictable information such as birthdays, pet names, or common words. Instead, use a passphrase composed of multiple unrelated words, numbers, and special characters. Never reuse passwords across multiple platforms, particularly between your personal email and online banking portal.

2. Enable Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-Factor Authentication adds an essential second barrier against unauthorized logins. Whenever available, enable MFA using authenticator apps or security keys rather than SMS codes alone. Even if a bad actor compromises your password, they cannot access your account without your secondary device.

3. Recognize and Avoid Phishing Scams

Be cautious of unsolicited emails, phone calls, or text messages claiming to be from your bank. Legitimate financial institutions will never ask for your PIN, complete card details, or login credentials via an unprompted message. Always verify suspicious requests directly through official banking channels.

4. Avoid Public Wi-Fi for Banking

Public Wi-Fi networks in cafes, hotels, and airports are often unencrypted and susceptible to interception. Avoid accessing your banking portal or entering credit card details while connected to public networks. Use your mobile cellular data or a trusted Virtual Private Network (VPN) instead.

5. Update Devices and Monitor Accounts

Regularly update your operating systems, browsers, and mobile banking applications to ensure security vulnerabilities are patched. Set up real-time transaction notifications on your mobile app so you can immediately detect and report any unauthorized charges.
